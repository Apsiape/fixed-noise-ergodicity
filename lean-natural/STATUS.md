## Statement fidelity (the FROZEN `Statements.lean`, sha256 `3e4f73e3…`)

Printed (Theorem D, `mainD`). Put `K = 16⁴`, `q = 5K`, `h = 4K` and `ε_* = (2(18q)²)^{-200}`. For
every finite Wang set `R`, a cardinal checker followed by an `h`-of-`q` quorum in five fixed
transverse boxes defines a rule `G_R` on `ℤ⁶` with alphabet `R ⊔ {⊥}`. At every `0 < ε ≤ ε_*`,
uniform whole-symbol replacement after `G_R` is ergodic if and only if `R` does not tile the plane.
If `R` tiles, there are two distinct translation-invariant stationary laws.

Printed (Theorem A, `mainA`). There is a total computable map `M ↦ W_M` from Turing machines to
finite Wang sets such that, for every `ε ∈ (0, ε_*]`, the positive-rate automaton obtained from
`G_{W_M}` is ergodic if and only if `M` halts.

## Proof-route notes (fidelity of the proof, not of the statement)

* **F1.** The non-tiling direction follows the paper's qualitative complete-history route
  ("Complete histories: the qualitative route"; the noise-diagram core's Lemma 8.5 for
  ergodicity), not the quantitative forbidden-square route (Lemmas `nw:forbidden`,
  `nw:mortality`, `nw:allinput`, `nw:ergodic`). The tiling is read off directly from the coherent
  letters of a complete history (`tiles_of_UL`); no compactness or legal-square argument is needed.
* **F2.** Sparse roots in a weak form: `P(v ∈ 𝓜) ≤ Σ_{f≥1}(K_c ε)^f ≤ 1/4` (`root_bound`) instead
  of `< 2ε`; the memory gap is `1/2` instead of `1 - 4ε`. Enough for non-ergodicity and for the
  two translation-invariant laws (`P(⊥) ≤ 1/4` against `P(⊥) ≥ 3/4`).
* **F3.** The coherence induction (`coh_rel`) uses the uniform age condition `s + d < t` for the
  displacements `0, e₁, e₂`. The core applies it to complete trajectories, where any age is
  available.
* **F4.** The marked field is the noise-diagram core's raw marked set (`UND.Coh.marked`), literally
  the paper's (`nw:mark`); its contour, packing and counting lemmas are reused; the base is
  `K_c ε ≤ 1/8` (`Const.Kc_mul_le`). Only the qualitative consequence (no infinite path a.s.) is
  formalized.
* **F5.** The memory branch evolves both starts with the two-sided noise from time `0`
  (`UND.CA.flow`), identified with the one-sided process by `noiseLaw₂_map_tau` and `flow_eq_run`.
* **F6.** Non-ergodicity is obtained from memory between the two Dirac starts
  (`UND.CA.not_ergodic_of_memoryFrom`); the translation-invariant laws are a separate clause (F7).
* **F7.** The translation-invariant laws: the paper first averages the Dirac law at a tiling
  over planar squares (a shift-invariant law on the tiling space), clones it transversely, then
  takes temporal Cesàro limits. The formalization exchanges the two averages: the temporal Cesàro
  limits from the clone and from blank are stationary laws with the one-site bounds at every site
  (`Avg.exists_stationary_le`); each is then averaged along the six coordinate directions in turn
  (one-dimensional averages `savg`, weak subsequential limits; `Avg.exists_TI`). Stationarity
  survives each average because the kernel commutes with translations
  (`UND.CA.law_map_shift`) and is Feller (`UND.CA.PhiBCF`); the bounds survive because they hold at
  every site and one-site cylinders are clopen. No averaging over squares, no tiling space and no
  boundary count is needed (each direction is a telescoping sum).
* **F8.** The domino reduction (the classical input of the paper's proof of Theorem A) is proved in
  `Reduction.lean` with the forgetting core's family `R_M`, not with Berger's or Robinson's
  construction; the statement (`DominoReduction`) does not name the construction. Theorem A is then
  proved without hypothesis (`FNR.theorem_A := FNR.ergodicity_reduction`).
* **F9.** "Refutable ⇒ not tiling" for `R_M` is obtained from the forgetting core's Theorem B
  at `ε₀` (ergodic when `M` halts, non-ergodic when `R_M` tiles), not from a combinatorial lemma.
